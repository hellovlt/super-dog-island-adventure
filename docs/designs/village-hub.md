# Design: The Village — a home the child builds, where friends gather before setting off

## Problem Statement

Multiplayer today is a five-letter code that drops friends straight onto an island.
There is no place to simply be together. Nowhere to meet before deciding where to go,
nothing to show a friend, no moment of "we are all here, let's go" — the thing that
makes playing with friends feel like playing with friends rather than sharing a level.

The player asked for a village: a common place where super dogs meet, connect, and then
set off together to open the islands.

## What Makes This Cool

1. **The village is the child's own.** Empty plots fill with houses, trampolines, a
   fountain and a tree, bought with bones. Their drawings hang as banners. It is the
   first part of the game that is theirs rather than authored.
2. **It remembers what they did.** Every rescued friend moves in and walks around. Every
   defeated giant leaves a statue beside the gate of the island it guarded. Stars sit on
   a shelf. Progress stops being a number in a menu and becomes a place.
3. **Leaving together is a moment.** Five gates ring the village, one per island. Stand
   in a gate and a flag rises over your dog. When everyone who can enter that island is
   standing in its gate, a 3-2-1 countdown fires and the party travels together.

## Constraints

- **No chat, ever.** Names come from a fixed list; communication is barking, jumping and
  gestures. This is the safety model, not a missing feature.
- **Private by decision.** The village is reachable only with the five-letter code. No
  strangers, no public square, no matchmaking (chosen explicitly over a public plaza).
- Plain ES modules, Three.js, no bundler, no server. WebRTC P2P via Trystero, 8 players
  max (`MAX_PLAYERS`, `multiplayer3d.js:5`).
- Ships to GitHub Pages; must keep working offline as an installed PWA.
- The 179-test suite is the gate. Every new rule gets a test that fails without it.
- **Players on older cached builds are live.** The game auto-updates, but a child can be
  mid-session on the previous build, so every packet change must degrade safely.

## Premises

1. No chat and no free text — barks, jumps and gestures only.
2. The village reuses the same physics, renderer and netcode, but **not the same
   bookkeeping**: it never writes campaign progress. See "The village must not touch the
   save" below — this is the rule the whole design hangs on.
3. A private village is empty by default, so it must grow with progress and be
   buildable. Otherwise it is a corridor the child runs through once.
4. Drawings stay visible only to code-friends; sharing is already opt-in.
5. The party travels together, but progress stays personal — bones and rescues are not
   pooled. Two children at different points in the campaign share a village.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Village as a side room

A sixth entry in the level picker, entered from a button. Rejected: a meeting place you
must deliberately navigate to does not become a meeting place.

### Approach B: Village as the front door

Press Play, land in the village, walk to a gate to reach an island, with growth decor and
two-player games. Rejected as the final scope only because it stops one step short of the
thing with the most pull.

### Approach C: Front door plus building (chosen)

Everything in B, plus plots the child fills with props bought with bones, and drawings
that become banners. Chosen: it gives bones a second purpose after the wardrobe and turns
the hub into something the child made.

## Recommended Approach

Approach C, staged so each step is playable on its own.

### The village is a place, not a sixth level

The obvious design — add a sixth `LEVELS` entry and call it world 5 — was tried on paper
and rejected after an adversarial review checked it against the code. The level number is
clamped to 0-4 in seven places (`adventure3d.js:20`, `:22` twice, `:36`, `:58`, `:59`,
`multiplayer3d.js:37`), `selectLevel` refuses any level at or above `unlocked` so the hub
could never be entered, and three tests assert the five-world shape
(`tests/campaign3d.test.mjs:62` for five distinct bosses, `tests/multiplayer3d.test.mjs:30`
for the level clamp, `tests/save-progress.test.mjs:4` via `hasProgress`, which counts any
`save.level>0` as an adventure in progress).

So: **`game.place` ('island' | 'village') is orthogonal to `game.level`.** In the village
the level still names the island the child would return to. Nothing about the campaign's
numbering changes, no clamp moves, and all 179 tests stay green as they are.

- `createVillage()` lives in a new `village3d.js` and returns a **complete world-shaped
  object**. The renderer destructures a fixed shape at load (`game3d.js:17`) and then
  reads `COURSES`, `LANDMARKS`, `NPCS`, `RUSH`, `EGGS` and `HAZARDS` unconditionally, so
  the village supplies all of them — mostly empty arrays plus a real `RUSH` stone,
  `solidsFor`, and a full `meta` (scale, sky, ground, sea, stone, accent). This is the
  one place where premise 2 costs real work rather than saving it.
- The boss is suppressed in the village: `BOSS_FILE` (`game3d.js:241`) is indexed by
  level and the boss HUD, danger ring and arena are island-only. In the village they are
  hidden rather than made conditional per-feature.
- `objective` and the zone label are village-specific ("Your village. Five gates, five
  islands."), instead of the island's "Rescue friends: 0 / 3".
- `village3d.js` goes into `sw.js` `ASSETS` with a `VERSION` bump. `tests/pwa.test.mjs:23`
  walks the import graph and fails if it is missing, which is the intended guard.

### The village must not touch the save

`snapshot()` (`adventure3d.js:37`) always rewrites `progress[this.level]` from the live
`collected/rescued/defeated/checkpoint/won` sets, and `restore()` filters those sets
against the current world's contents. A village world is mostly empty, so a shared
instance would write an empty `progress[level]` on the very first `storeSave()` — and
`launch()` ends in `storeSave()` (`game3d.js:485`), so **pressing Play into the village
would be enough to re-lock the islands the child had already beaten**. `get unlocked`
(`adventure3d.js:36`) walks `validWin` from zero and collapses. This is the same failure
as ISSUE-005 (commit `4abc246`), one layer deeper.

The rule, which every later step depends on:

- While `place==='village'`, `storeSave()` copies `progress` and `challenges` through
  **verbatim** from the last island snapshot. The village never regenerates them.
- The village's own state (plots, village Bone Rush best) lives under a separate `village`
  key in the save, never in `progress[level]` or `challenges[level]`. The existing load
  filters (`/^[0-4]$/` for challenges, `adventure3d.js:21`) already reject anything else,
  so an old build reading a new save simply ignores the village.
- Test, written before step 1 is called done: enter the village, run Bone Rush, buy a hat,
  reload, and assert `progress` and `challenges` are byte-identical to before.

### Who owns `place`

`game3d.js` owns it. `Adventure3D` stays a campaign object that knows nothing about the
village; the village world is swapped into `game.world` with its own spawn, solids and
empty entity lists.

One deliberate exception, found while building step 1: falling in the water cost a heart
even in the village, because `tick` applies fall damage to every world. A fence is not an
answer, since children jump fences. So the simulation gained one general rule, not a
village special case: a world may be marked `safe`, and in a safe world a fall costs
nothing and a bubble carries the player back. Eight words in `adventure3d.js:126`, covered
by a test that runs a dog out of the village in twenty-four directions.

`createVillage()` must therefore return everything the renderer destructures at load
(`game3d.js:17`: `PLATFORMS, FRIENDS, KEYS, STARS, BONES, CHECKPOINTS, SPAWN, TREES,
PROPS, ROCKS`), everything read later (`COURSES` as an array, `LANDMARKS`, `NPCS`, `RUSH`,
`EGGS`, `HAZARDS`, optional `ICE`), the solids the simulation needs (`STATIC_SOLIDS`,
`CAGE_SOLIDS`, `ENEMIES`, `solidsFor`), and a full `meta` — `name`, `subtitle`, `boss`,
`color` are all read by `updateCampaignUI`, the level label and the boss name, not just
the colour and scale fields.

Also village-specific, because each is unconditional today: the minimap (`drawMap`,
`game3d.js:507`, hardcodes the island, arena and bridge rectangles), the boss HUD and
danger ring, the objective line, and the zone label.

### Travelling together, across a page reload

`changeLevel` (`game3d.js:298`) ends in `location.reload()`, and `party` is a module-local
`let` (`game3d.js:314`) whose room code is never persisted. Left alone, the countdown
would end with all eight children dropping out of the room — the headline feature
destroying itself.

The party code is therefore persisted beside the existing autostart flag
(`sessionStorage`, read at `game3d.js:567`), and the boot path rejoins automatically.
Three details the mechanism does not give for free:

- **Host identity must survive the trip.** Today the only host/joiner distinction is the
  module-local `partyJoined` (`game3d.js:314`), so rejoining everyone as a joiner would
  leave a party with no host at all, and "the village belongs to whoever started it" would
  have nothing to point at. The host flag is persisted with the code, and `host` also goes
  into the look packet so a visitor knows whose village to draw.
- **Rejoin is a third mode, not a join.** `startParty(...,{joined:true})` arms the lonely
  timer (`game3d.js:329`), which after 18 seconds would tell children who just travelled
  together that nobody is on that island and they should check the code. Rejoin suppresses
  it.
- **The stored code is cleared** in `leaveParty()` (`game3d.js:348`) and on "Start over"
  (`game3d.js:489`), which also reloads. Otherwise a child who left a party silently
  rejoins it on the next reload.

Children see a short "finding your friends again" moment on arrival, then the party
re-forms. Removing the reload entirely is a bigger refactor (the whole Three.js scene is
built once at module scope) and is a follow-up. **Measure the rejoin latency with eight
peers before building the gates** — if it runs past a few seconds, the no-reload refactor
moves from follow-up to prerequisite.

### Gates, readiness, and who decides

- A gate is a pad plus an arch, one per island, locked-looking when that island is not
  unlocked **for the viewer**.
- Standing on a pad marks that player ready. Readiness travels as a spare bit in the
  existing flags bitfield of the state packet (bit 16 is free, `multiplayer3d.js:31`),
  not as a new field: `packState` returns a positional six-number array and
  `tests/multiplayer3d.test.mjs:25` asserts that length.
- **Unlock mismatch is the expected case, not an edge case.** A gate only counts players
  who have that island unlocked. Nothing in the state packet carries a peer's progress, so
  eligibility travels in the **look packet** instead: it gains `unlocked` (0-5) and a small
  `village` summary. `unpackLook` (`multiplayer3d.js:41`) reads named fields only and
  ignores the rest, so old clients are unaffected. Without this a client cannot tell
  "cannot come" from "has not walked over yet", and the countdown either never fires or
  fires early.
- A visitor who lacks the unlock sees a dark arch and a readable reason; the countdown
  runs for everyone who can go and they stay behind in the village rather than silently
  failing (`selectLevel`, `adventure3d.js:58`, returns false for a locked level).
- No host authority: every client computes readiness from its own roster view each frame.
  The countdown starts when the local view says everyone eligible is on the same pad,
  aborts if anyone steps off or leaves, and a peer who joins mid-countdown does not
  interrupt it. Because all clients see the same pads, they converge; a straggler whose
  packet was stale simply starts a beat later.
- Alone, standing on a pad starts the island after 1.5 seconds.

### Old builds must not see ghosts

`unpackState` clamps an unknown level into 0-4 (`multiplayer3d.js:37`). A village dog
broadcasting naively would be drawn by an old client as standing in *their* island at
village coordinates, and listed in the roster as being on Sky Castle.

Village state packets are therefore deliberately out of range for the old validator: the
packet carries `y + 1000` (one named constant, shared by both ends), which makes an old
client's `unpackState` return null and skip the friend entirely (`multiplayer3d.js:36`,
then `:82` drops the message). Nothing is drawn, nothing is claimed, no ghost, no console
output. New clients test the village flag before the range check and subtract the offset.

Bit allocation, since two different bits are needed: **bit 16 = ready**, **bit 32 = in the
village**. Both are free today — `packState` sets only 1|2|4|8 and `unpackState` masks the
same four, so an old client drops both silently. `packState(player,level)` gains a third
argument for the village flag.

What the old build actually shows, since a success criterion depends on it: the traveller
stays listed in the roster as "arriving…" (`game3d.js:359`) and their dog is never drawn.
Not silence, but nothing false.

**Friends must be co-located by place, not by level.** `drawFriends` hides any friend
whose `state.level !== game.level` (`game3d.js:195`). Two children in the village who are
at different points in the campaign have different `game.level` values, so as the code
stands today neither would see the other — the headline feature would render nothing. The
rule becomes: both in the village means together, whatever their levels; otherwise the
existing level match applies. The roster line gains an "In the village" case instead of
`LEVELS[level].name`.

### Growth, from the save that already exists

Nothing new is stored for the child's own village: rescued friends in `progress[].rescued`
become NPC dogs with houses, each `progress[].won` puts a statue beside that island's
gate, stars go on a shelf, bones and secrets on the signpost.

A **visitor** cannot read any of that — it lives in the host's save, not in any packet. So
the look packet's `village` summary carries what a visitor needs to draw the place:
`{plots, rescued, won, stars, host}`, a few dozen bytes, sent on change rather than in the
80 ms state loop.

The drawing hangs as a banner, with one catch: `myLook()` (`game3d.js:317`) transmits the
image only when `settings.shareDrawing` **and** the drawing is worn as a cape. A banner is
not a cape, so either the condition widens or the banner gets its own opt-in. Because this
governs what leaves the device, it is an explicit decision, not an implementation detail.

### Whose village visitors see

The village belongs to whoever started the party; visitors walk around the host's village,
with the host's houses, statues and banner. Showing a friend your place is the point, so
this is decided, not open: alone or hosting you stand in your own village, and visiting
means seeing someone else's.

### Building

Plots are fixed anchor points. A catalogue of props (house, trampoline, fountain, tree,
lamp, flag) with bone prices, bought and placed in a build mode that reuses the wardrobe's
shop pattern and the drawing studio's touch handling. Stored as
`village:{plots:{<plotId>:<propId>}}` in the existing save and sent to friends **in the
look packet only** (which is sent on change, not in the 80 ms state loop). Unknown prop
ids are ignored on load, so a save from a newer build never breaks an older one.

### Mini-games

The Bone Rush stone already exists per level (`campaign3d.js:113`) and is reused in the
village as-is. Tag is cut from this scope to a follow-up: it is new synced game state
across a full mesh, it serves none of the success criteria, and the village works without it.

## Open Questions

1. **Does the child want to land in the village every time?** The menu keeps a "straight
   to the island" shortcut. If the village is not used as a hub after a week, flip the
   default back and keep it as a place they visit.
2. **How long is the rejoin gap after travelling?** Measured, step 0: two peers both
   reloading re-formed the party in **2.9s and 5.9s** on top of roughly two seconds of page
   load. Workable, so the reload stays for now, but it is at the edge of what a child will
   sit through and the arrival must say plainly that it is looking for friends. Re-measure
   with more than two peers before step 3 is called done.
3. **How many plots, and what do props cost?** Prices should be readable next to the
   wardrobe's, and cheap enough that the first house arrives the same day.
4. **Does the build mode work with a finger on a tablet?** The drawing studio's handling
   is the reference, but placement is a different gesture from drawing.

## Success Criteria

- Press Play and stand in the village with no menu step, within a second of the Play
  click, measured from the click to `$('loading').hidden=true` in the dev browser used for
  QA.
- A friend joins by code and sees the host's village: the host's houses, statues, banner,
  and their own dog beside them.
- Two dogs stand in the same gate, the countdown runs, both land on the island together,
  and the party re-forms there without anyone touching the friends screen.
- A player who has not unlocked that island sees a dark arch and a readable reason, and
  the others can still go.
- The first prop is affordable within one island's worth of bones, is there after a
  reload, and a visiting friend sees it. (Exact prices close Open Question 3.)
- Every rescued friend and defeated giant already in the child's save appears in their
  village with no action from them.
- A client running commit `9a52dba` (the build live before this work), in a party with a
  village player, draws no ghost and logs no error; the villager shows as "arriving…".
- Entering the village, running Bone Rush and buying a hat leaves `progress` and
  `challenges` byte-identical — the ISSUE-005 class of bug cannot recur.
- Two children at different campaign points stand in the village and see each other.
- All 179 existing tests still pass unchanged, plus new tests for: gate readiness and
  countdown, unlock mismatch at a gate, village packets rejected by the old validator and
  accepted by the new one, save round-trip with and without a village, co-location by place
  rather than level, and unknown prop ids.

   the plan below shifts.
1. **Village world, solo.** `village3d.js` with a complete world shape, `game.place` owned
   by `game3d.js`, the save guard and its test **first**, boss and island HUD suppressed,
   village minimap, spawn, signpost, five gates with per-viewer lock state, Play lands
   there, menu shortcut and a way back from an island, `sw.js` updated with a `VERSION`
   bump. Playable end to end.
2. **Growth decor.** Friends, statues, stars, banner — all read from the existing save.
3. **Party in the village.** Village and ready bits, old-client rejection, co-location by

4. **Building.** Catalogue, prices, build mode, save shape, sync to visitors. *This is the

5. **QA pass** with two browsers, then deploy.

## Reviewer Concerns

Two rounds of independent review against the code, scored 6/10 both times, each round
finding something that would have broken in the child's hands:

- Round 1 killed the sixth-`LEVELS`-entry plan (seven level clamps, three failing tests),
  the reload that would have destroyed the party, and the ghost-player consequence of the
  level clamp.
- Round 2 found that a shared `Adventure3D` in the village would erase campaign progress
  on the first Play (`snapshot()` rewrites `progress[level]` from live sets), that friends
  at different campaign points would be invisible to each other (`state.level===game.level`),
  and that gate eligibility was not computable from anything in the packets.

Open and accepted rather than solved:

- Rejoin latency with eight peers is unmeasured; step 0 exists to settle it before the
  gates are built.
- The banner needs the drawing share rule widened, which changes what leaves the device.
- Two children can each be looking at a different village (host's vs their own) depending
  on who started the party; the rule is decided but has not been played with a real child.
